## The assist, augment, automate ladder

Classify each use case by how much it does on its own. An assist drafts; an augment decides with a human; an automate executes. Not everything should climb the ladder, and not everyone wants it to. The governance consequence is proportional: more autonomy means more rigor, because the system initiates actions, triggers workflows, and executes tasks with GxP impact.

## What the ladder changes on the autonomous rung

The intended-use lecture lands the same claim from the output side: advisory outputs shift the burden to proving a trained human is in the loop, while autonomous outputs demand harder confidence scoring, fallback logic, and failure-mode containment for the excursion that will eventually come.
