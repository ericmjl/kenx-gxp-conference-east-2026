## The citation record is a human record

FDA warning letters rose 15.9 percent to 303 in the last fiscal year; 21 CFR 211.22(d), the responsibilities of the quality control unit, was cited 184 times and has been the top citation four years running. Studies attribute more than 80 percent of process deviations to humans. The organization, never the AI, receives the warning letter, so the productive fear is not out-of-control AI but the unglamorous, citation-generating reality of human error across documentation, investigation, change control, oversight, data integrity, and the validation lifecycle.

## The 2026 cases make the point concrete

The Purolea warning letter of April 2, 2026, the first AI-related CGMP citation, turned on the defense that "AI did not tell us"; Applied Therapeutics lost data for 47 subjects to vendor deletion; Tentamus India involved falsification. None of these are stories of misbehaving models. They are stories of missing human gates.

## Defensible AI control is human-centric design

Five properties make an AI control defensible: documented intended use including its limits, risk-proportionate continuous validation, ALCOA+ audit trails extended to the AI layer (user, model and version, inputs, outputs, justification, confidence), named humans at every approval gate rather than rubber-stamp human-in-the-loop, and lifecycle monitoring. The governance plan fits in three steps: inventory all AI including shadow AI, enforce a human gate before anything enters a GxP record, and treat validation as a continuous lifecycle. The regulatory convergence of 2025 and 2026, the FDA AI credibility guidance, the ISPE GAMP AI guide, the EU GMP Annex 22 draft, and the joint FDA/EMA principles, means the excuses are gone. As Bharanitharan Rajendran closed: untrained humans fail inspections, and untrained AI fails patients.
