## The etiquette that makes the war room work

Never make anything up. If you do not know, say so, or say "let me double check that for you", which is itself the trigger for the war room to find and confirm the documentation so the scribe can lean over with the evidence ready. Inspectors read SOPs in silence and then demand the process executed live; seniority has nothing to do with who demonstrates it, because the SOP names the operator. Once an auditor finds a thread they keep pulling, and you cannot guess where it goes, so guessing is the only unrecoverable move. This is the operator's phrase inside [[Regulatory inspections verify execution against SOPs, not the existence of documents]].

## Preparedness is scheduled, not heroic

Larger organizations staff a war room with SMEs watching a live transcription, predicting the investigator's next path, and assembling documents before they are requested. Mock audits run at least once per functional area per year, typically by outside firms staffed with former FDA or EMA auditors. The practitioner's reassurance: audits are long days but fun, as long as you know your process. Guessing also breaks the patient-facing promise in [[GxP documentation builds institutional trust on behalf of patients]], and it is the inspection-room version of [[Companies own the accuracy of AI-drafted submissions regardless of the drafting tool]]: unverified words still belong to the company. [[Regulators audit against principles, so size quality rigor to risk]] does not change the etiquette; it only changes how much evidence the war room should already have pulled.
