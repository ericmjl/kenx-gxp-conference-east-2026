Gargano's framing statistic: at a top-five client, employees using AI more than eight hours a day grew from 600 in 2024 to 40,000 in 2025 to nearly 110,000 through July 2026, and he says AI now drives 95 percent of his own office day. His core claim is that AI is a force multiplier, never a replacement: it extrapolates from data outside your company, so blindly applying its output is the user's failure, and since it is easier to edit than create, the winners will be the people who get efficient at directing the edit. Gargano's mantra is that validation is not a document-based process but a data-based one; he mocks the practice of wrapping vendor documentation after a two-day, three-person review of a three-million-line product, and notes Oracle IQ certificates are auto-generated templates with about twelve variables plugged in. The FDA's number one focus is data integrity, specifically trend analysis across deviations and failures, followed by what the data says the organization should improve, which is exactly the work validation staff should be redeployed into Quality to do. He reads a real FDA warning letter citing AI agents used to generate specs, procedures, and master production control records without Quality Unit review and calls it a process failure rather than an AI failure, and explains AI drift as model performance changing as the model keeps learning. Numbers cited: about 80 percent of top-100 pharma use AI in validation; the largest pharma spent 3.8 billion dollars on its own LLM, reports about 60 percent confidence in its results, and laid off 60 percent of its validation team; a client with 27 global sites saves 2.4 million dollars per site per year after a 3 million dollar investment with zero headcount reduction; RCM's standing offer is a 65 percent cost cut and throughput time more than halved within 18 months.
