## Summary

> Twenty-four months of building and validating AI-assisted applications for pharmacovigilance and IT compliance, told as lessons learned. The validation paradigm shifted from deterministic CSV (known input, predefined output) to AI-assisted decision support (known evidence, acceptable behavior range), operationalized as a six-step GxP process: controlled evidence, fixed questions and prompts, AI analysis, evidence plus rationale, human review, approved output with audit trail. The team wrote dedicated SOPs for the AI SDLC, testing, and validation; built golden sets and baselines with three consecutive regression runs and delta views; hardened guardrail language so the system answers "unable to verify" when evidence is missing; and catalogued failure modes including severity inflation, duplicate findings, plausible but unevidenced assumptions, and citation overreach.

### Knowledge Points

#### 1. Cultural and Ethical Foundations for AI in GxP

- Responsible AI mindset: Educate AI teams on FDA principles and GxP priorities (traceability, guardrails, patient-centricity); embed fairness, transparency, explainability, privacy/data governance, reliability, monitoring, safety, and security.
- Compliance culture: Shift from pure efficiency to defensible processes; normalize GxP terminology and rationale for controls within engineering teams; human experts retain final decision authority.

#### 2. Data Security and Environmental Guardrails

- Containment and isolation: Keep all data within dedicated AWS instances; enforce tenant isolation with per-client databases; least-privilege IAM, continuous monitoring, auditability, backups, and controlled change management.
- Controlled agent behavior: Prohibit public web access; require agents to rely on authorized sources; iteratively harden guardrails and operationalize secure behavior.

#### 3. Reframing Validation and GxP Testing for AI

- From deterministic CSV to AI-assisted validation: Validate acceptable behavior ranges grounded in evidence and decision relevance, not exact wording.
- <mark style="background-color: #F9EDD4">Six-step GxP process: Controlled Evidence → Fixed Questions & Prompts → AI Analysis → Evidence + Rationale → Human Review → Approved Output + Audit Trail.</mark>
- OQ model and acceptance criteria: Test for materially consistent facts, evidence basis, status, and conclusions; display citations, rationale, and confidence; document deviations; defer protocol approval until repeated controlled runs build confidence.
- <mark style="background-color: #F9EDD4">Dedicated SOPs: Create separate SOPs for AI SDLC, testing, and validation to reduce deviations and clarify AI-specific procedures.</mark>

#### 4. Pharmacovigilance Use Cases, Client Variability, and Training

- PV drafting and analytics: Support DSURs, annual summaries, SAE/DSCR reports, RTQs; extract data to Excel; generate visualizations and trend insights.
- Client-specific templates and tonality: Customize outputs per client; preserve stylistic fidelity and citation handling.
- Modality and terminology differences: Train separate agents for small molecule oncology vs. CGT/biologics; avoid cross-use without retraining; normalize terminology (e.g., AEs like nausea variants).
- Discovery and documentation: Engage PV scientists to catalog internal vocabularies and report structures; cannot reuse validation artifacts across clients; invest upfront to align on expectations.

#### 5. Testing Strategy, Baselines, Golden Sets, and Regression

- Collaborative test development: Co-create scripts with SMEs and QA; handle synonyms and domain nuances; target precise queries (dose/country comparisons across indications).
- Baselines and golden sets: Establish evidence selection baselines and golden sets to calibrate acceptable outputs; iterate 3–6 months to refine guardrails and protocols.
- Regression and negative testing: Run multiple consecutive runs; detect unchanged vs. changed evidence; show delta views; prompt no-change warnings; validate platform core plus client-specific behaviors.

#### 6. Failure Modes and Remediation

- <mark style="background-color: #F9EDD4">Common issues: Severity inflation, false deficiencies from missing evidence, plausible but unevidenced assumptions, citation overreach/misapplication (Part 11/Annex 11/GAMP), duplicate findings, false precision, lifecycle/context misinterpretations.</mark>
- Guardrails and language: Replace definitive deficiency claims with “unable to verify” when evidence is missing; constrain severity classification to evidenced impact; ensure findings are editable with rationale and basis.
- Deduplication and normalization: Cluster repeated findings; unify technical terms to prevent splitting identical concepts; constrain regulatory references to relevant contexts; incorporate lifecycle-aware checks (e.g., first PR not a deviation).

#### 7. Outcomes, Agent Persona Model, and Architecture

- <mark style="background-color: #F9EDD4">Efficiency with accountability: Rapid evidence triage and drafting; cross-document reviews surface gaps/themes; traceable citations enable quick verification; human-in-the-loop decisions preserved.</mark>
