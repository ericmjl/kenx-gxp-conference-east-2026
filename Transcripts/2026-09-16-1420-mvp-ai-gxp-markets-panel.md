# Panel on MVP, AI, and GxP-regulated markets

> Recorded 2026-09-16 at 1420 via Plaud. 43:27.

## Summary

> Quality and product leaders on what a minimum viable product means in GxP markets: moderator Bryan Ennis (head of quality at Salesforce), Courtney Steele (Director of Product Management, DeepHow), and Robin Stolzberg (Global Head of Quality, Sapio Sciences). The consensus: the MVP is the customer's intended use, and AI changes none of it; vendors owe full functional testing while sponsors validate intended use in their own context of use, because a vendor cannot assess patient risk without it; risk assessment should be objective and tool-driven, since humans on the hook for outcomes are incentivized to dilute ratings; and compliance belongs in the product from inception (audit trails, roles and permissions, e-signature triggers with re-authentication and SSO) rather than retrofitted.
>
> Steele described DeepHow's route to GxP readiness through a separate validated platform with deep auditability (who changed what, when, in which time zone) and configurable AI instead of product forks; Stolzberg argued for quality-by-design from inception with just-in-time compliance guidance for product teams. Emerging MVP expectations named by the panel: AI-native interactions, embedded change management with automated change-boundary logs, headless architectures, accessibility documentation, and a drift toward continuous validation.

2. GXP Strategies: Regulated markets require audit trails, time zone accuracy, value-change tracking, roles/permissions, and compliant Part 11 implementations; build compliance into the product from the start to avoid costly retrofits.
3. Configurability Over Forking: Use configurable settings to enable/disable features for compliance, avoiding separate AI models or product forks.
4. Shared Validation Responsibility: Vendors must perform comprehensive functional testing; sponsors validate intended use and context, per ICH Q9 thinking.

• Key Insight: Manual, individual-led risk assessments are often diluted because people are reluctant to personally own the identified risks, leading to ineffective results.
• Risk Identified: If a vendor's risk-based testing approach is misaligned with the client's needs, the validation burden and cost for the client significantly increases.
• Decision: The consensus is that vendors should perform comprehensive testing, providing a robust foundation for the client to then apply their own risk-based approach for their specific implementation.
