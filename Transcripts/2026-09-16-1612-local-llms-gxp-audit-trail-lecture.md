## Summary

### 2. Local vs. Cloud/Frontier Deployment Tradeoffs for GxP

- **Local/Open-Weight Deployment:**
  - **Advantages:** Offers greater control, ensuring data stays within the organization's boundary. It allows for freezing and fingerprinting exact model weights and runtime environments for reproducibility. The organization operates and schedules workloads on its own infrastructure.
  - **Disadvantages:** Requires hardware investment and internal operational effort. The primary technical limitation is the finite context window of the models.
- **Cloud/Frontier Service (e.g., Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini):**
  - **Advantages:** Provides access to the most advanced models and integrated tools without the user managing compute infrastructure.
  - **Disadvantages:** Offers limited control and transparency. Data is sent to the provider, and it's difficult to know what's happening behind the scenes (e.g., model changes, throttling), creating uncertainty and impacting long-term explainability and repeatability.
- **Comparative Performance:** In experiments, cloud models showed variable performance. <mark style="background-color: #F9EDD4">Claude identified 4/4 completeness problems but had high false positives (37-43).</mark> ChatGPT identified 3-4/4 problems with fewer false positives (0-8). Gemini repeatedly failed the task.
- **Assurance and Evidence:** A key consideration is the evidence needed to explain a result 18 months later. The deployment choice directly affects the assurance package, with local deployments offering more operator-owned evidence and cloud services relying more on supplier evidence.

### 3. Framework for AI System Assurance and Control

- <mark style="background-color: #F9EDD4">**Three-Step Assurance Case Framework:**
  - **Step 1: Define "What Ran":** Document the exact conditions of an AI run, including intended use, input data, versioned prompts, and all configurations (model, runtime, RAG, tools).</mark>
  - **Step 2: Explain "How It Performed":** Provide evidence of performance, including raw outputs, source-to-claim traceability, metrics on coverage and false positives, and the human disposition of flagged items.
  - **Step 3: Detail "How It Stays Controlled":** Outline the ongoing governance, including change triggers, a risk-based regression testing strategy (using a "golden dataset"), continuous monitoring, and incident management.
- <mark style="background-color: #F9EDD4">**Four-Layer Validation Framework:** Performance depends on the entire workflow, not just the model.</mark> Assurance is needed at each layer:
  - **1. Input & Preparation:** Ensure complete and intact data input, with failures made obvious.
  - **2. Retrieval / RAG:** Assure retrieval quality, source control, and the end-to-end effect, as retrieval does not guarantee the model uses the context correctly.
  - **3. Model & Inference Runtime:** Assure version control, settings, and understand variability and failure modes. The inference engine itself (e.g., Ollama version) can significantly impact results.
  - **4. <mark style="background-color: #F9EDD4">Tools & Human Review:** Assure agent behavior, traceability, and robust human oversight.</mark>
- **Evaluation Pipeline Solidity:** The system used to evaluate and score models must be deterministic and solid, for instance, by using regex for scoring instead of another LLM, which could introduce bias.

### <mark style="background-color: #F9EDD4">4. Unresolved Strategic Choices and Potential Improvements</mark>

- **Structuring Human Review:** A key challenge is designing a human-in-the-loop (HITL) process that effectively complements the AI to catch what it misses, without duplicating the entire manual review effort.
- **Control vs. Capability:** Organizations must make a defensible trade-off between using local models for more control versus higher-capability vendor models that create supplier dependency.
- **Defining "Good Enough" Performance:** Determining when an AI's performance is sufficient depends on risk, comparison to human performance, and the consequences of error. Experiments inform this decision but do not make it.
- **Potential Improvements:**
  - **Low-hanging fruit:** Adjusting model parameters (e.g., temperature) and re-running against a golden dataset.
  - **Further enhancements:** Fine-tuning the model (noted as increasingly accessible), using a better RAG system, and upgrading hardware (e.g., to NVIDIA GPUs) to run larger models.
  - **Model Knowledge:** LLMs may have foundational knowledge of regulated environments from public data. Performance can be improved by using RAG to provide specific context (like a "QA rulebook") or by using system prompts to frame the AI's role (e.g., "You are a GXP expert").
